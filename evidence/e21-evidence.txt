SCOPE NOTE
==========
E21's full scope (retro-pay, arrears, GL posting, 100% calculation
coverage, and consolidating two overlapping modules) is far larger
than this session's remaining budget. Investigated "statutory-correct
payroll" directly by reading runPayroll() (advanced-hr.service.ts,
the actual payroll calculation engine) in full, and found a real,
critical, cleanly-provable bug: no protection against re-running an
already-paid period, silently double-paying every employee. Also
investigated a money-arithmetic angle (float vs Decimal) but, unlike
prior sessions' findings, could NOT construct a genuine FAIL-first
proof of business-observable harm from it - documented honestly below
rather than overclaimed. Fixed the double-pay bug completely and
correctly, and files the honest remaining scope.

THE INVESTIGATION
==================
Read runPayroll() in full. Found: no check anywhere for whether a
PayrollRun already exists for the given period before creating a new
one and marking it PAID.

Separately investigated the money-arithmetic pattern (gross/deduction/
net summed via plain `+=`), matching D084/D085/D086's own precedent.
Confirmed a real, measurable float-accumulation artifact via direct
node repro (totalGross - totalDeductions differs from totalNet by
~1e-12 for 3 employees at 12.5% tax). HOWEVER: repeated attempts to
turn this into a genuine FAIL-first vitest assertion failed - every
comparison attempted (raw subtraction, toFixed rounding, toPrecision
string comparison, integer-cents rounding) either (a) reintroduced its
OWN float noise inside the test's assertion, invalidating the proof,
or (b) rounded away the drift entirely, since the drift stays below
1e-10 even at 1000 simulated employees for this specific arithmetic
shape (single multiply + subtract per row, summed) - unlike D084/D085/
D086 which involved EQUALITY/THRESHOLD CHECKS that concretely flip a
decision (accept/reject a journal, accept/reject a payment). Converted
the arithmetic to Prisma.Decimal anyway as defensive hardening
(matches CODE_STANDARDS), but did NOT claim a demonstrated business-
impact bug for this specific aspect - documented honestly in D095
rather than forcing a fragile or misleading test.

THE BUG, CONFIRMED (double-pay)
====================================
Seeded an existing PayrollRun with status "PAID" for period
2026-01-01 to 2026-01-31. Calling runPayroll() again for the identical
period succeeded, creating a second, fully independent PayrollRun with
its own PayrollSlips - every employee paid twice.

MECHANISM (this phase's own fix)
====================================
runPayroll() now queries for an existing PayrollRun matching
tenantId/periodStart/periodEnd with status "PAID" before doing any
work, and throws BadRequestException naming the period if found.
